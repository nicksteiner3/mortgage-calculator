How Much Down Payment Do You Need for a House?

The minimum down payment depends on the loan type. Putting down 20% is the usual target because it avoids PMI on conventional loans.

Minimum down payments by loan type

Loan typeTypical minimum down
Conventional (first-time buyers)3%
FHA3.5% with a credit score of 580 or higher
VA (eligible veterans)0%
USDA (eligible rural areas)0%

Lower minimums usually mean PMI or mortgage insurance premiums, so the monthly payment can be higher even with less cash up front.

Why 20% is the common target

On a conventional loan, 20% down means you don't pay PMI. On a $400,000 home, that's $80,000 down. Less down means a larger loan, more interest, and an added insurance cost until your balance falls to 78% to 80% of the home's value.

Closing costs are separate from the down payment and usually run 2% to 5% of the loan amount.

How the down payment changes your monthly payment

The down payment reduces the loan amount, and the loan amount drives the principal and interest payment. Using the standard formula with a 6.5% rate over 30 years, here is how three down payment levels compare on a $400,000 home:

Down paymentLoan amountPrincipal and interest
3% ($12,000)$388,000About $2,452
10% ($40,000)$360,000About $2,275
20% ($80,000)$320,000About $2,023

These payments do not include PMI, which is usually added when the down payment is under 20% on a conventional loan. A small down payment can look affordable at the closing table, but it can cost more each month and over the life of the loan.

Where down payment money can come from

Lenders need to see that the money is yours and that it came from an acceptable source. Common sources include:

  • Savings. Money you have saved in bank accounts or other liquid accounts is the most straightforward source. Lenders usually want a paper trail of statements for recent months.
  • Gift funds. Many loan programs allow a family member to give money for the down payment. The donor typically signs a gift letter stating the money doesn't need to be repaid.
  • Down payment assistance. Some state, county, and city programs offer grants or low-interest second loans. Eligibility depends on income, location, and whether you are a first-time buyer, so check local listings.
  • Retirement accounts. Some plans allow withdrawals or loans for a home purchase, but they can carry taxes, penalties, or lost growth. Review the tradeoffs carefully.

Avoid borrowing the down payment on credit cards or unsecured loans. Lenders may count that debt against you, and the monthly payments reduce how much you can qualify for.

Down payment is not the only upfront cost

Buyers often focus on the down payment and forget that closing costs are separate. Closing costs are commonly 2% to 5% of the loan amount. On a $320,000 loan, that range is roughly $6,400 to $16,000. Lenders also commonly expect reserves, which are savings left after closing, to show you can cover a few months of payments.

Add the down payment, closing costs, and a cushion for moving, repairs, and furnishing the home to get your total cash needed. That number is often more important than the down payment alone.

Choosing a down payment amount

There is no single correct amount. A larger down payment lowers the monthly payment and can remove PMI, but using all of your savings can leave you exposed to emergencies. A smaller down payment keeps cash available, but it increases the loan and may add insurance costs.

A practical approach is to set a target based on the monthly payment you can afford with taxes, insurance, and any PMI included. Then work backward to the down payment that makes that payment realistic. Keep an emergency fund separate from the money you put down.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy with less than 3% down? Some programs, including certain VA and USDA loans, can require no down payment for eligible buyers. Check the rules for the program you're considering.

Should I empty my savings for a bigger down payment? Usually not. Keeping an emergency fund helps you avoid high-cost borrowing if something goes wrong after you move in.

Do gift funds count toward the down payment? Often yes, when they are documented with a gift letter and the donor has no repayment expectation. Your lender sets the exact requirements.
