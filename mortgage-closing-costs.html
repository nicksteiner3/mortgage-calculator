Mortgage Closing Costs: What to Expect

Closing costs are the fees you pay to finish a home loan. They're usually 2% to 5% of the loan amount, paid on top of your down payment.

What closing costs usually include

  • Loan origination fee and underwriting fees
  • Appraisal and credit report fees
  • Title search, title insurance, and settlement or escrow fees
  • Recording fees and transfer taxes, which vary by state
  • Prepaid items such as homeowners insurance and property taxes held in escrow

How to lower them

Get a Loan Estimate from at least three lenders and compare the fee lines, not just the rate. You can ask the seller for a closing cost credit, which is common in slower markets. Some lenders also offer to cover part of the costs in exchange for a slightly higher rate. Ask whether that trade makes sense for how long you plan to keep the loan.

How much closing costs usually run

Closing costs are commonly 2% to 5% of the loan amount, but the exact figure depends on the loan, the price, and where you live. On a $320,000 loan, 2% is $6,400 and 5% is $16,000. Some buyers pay less, and some pay more when local transfer taxes or unusual title issues apply.

Closing costs are separate from the down payment, so plan for both. A common mistake is saving enough for the down payment and then finding out at the closing table that you still need several thousand dollars.

Understanding the paperwork

In the United States, lenders must give you a Loan Estimate within three business days after you apply. It lists the expected fees in standard categories and shows the estimated cash needed to close. Before closing, you should receive a Closing Disclosure at least three business days in advance. It shows the final loan terms and costs.

Compare the two documents line by line. Some fees have limits on how much they can increase between the estimate and the final figures, while others can change more freely. If a number has changed, ask the lender to explain why and to show the reason for the change in writing.

Common closing cost categories

  • Lender fees. These include origination or underwriting charges and any discount points you choose to buy.
  • Third-party services. Appraisal, credit report, flood certification, and inspection fees usually come from outside providers.
  • Title and settlement. Title search, title insurance, and the settlement or escrow agent's fee are common here.
  • Government fees. Recording fees and any transfer or mortgage taxes depend on where the property is located.
  • Prepaid items. Homeowners insurance premiums, property tax deposits for escrow, and interest from the closing date to the start of the first full month are often collected at closing.

Ways to lower what you pay

Shopping around can reduce your costs. Get a Loan Estimate from at least three lenders and compare the fee lines, not just the interest rate. A lower rate with higher fees may cost more than a slightly higher rate with lower fees, depending on how long you keep the loan.

You may also be able to ask for help. A seller credit, sometimes called a seller concession, can cover part of your closing costs, although limits depend on the loan type and down payment. Some lenders offer lender credits that reduce upfront costs in exchange for a higher rate. Ask whether the trade makes sense for how long you expect to keep the loan.

For title insurance and settlement services, compare quotes where you are allowed to choose the provider. Ask your agent or lender which fees are shoppable in your state.

Plan your cash to close

Make a simple checklist before you make an offer. Include your down payment, estimated closing costs, earnest money deposit, prepaid items, and a cushion for moving and immediate repairs. Confirm with your lender how your funds must be documented and how they should be transferred, since large unexplained deposits can delay approval.

Ask for the final figure in writing a few days before closing so you can arrange the funds in advance. Wire fraud is a real risk, so confirm wiring instructions by phone using a number you already have, not one from an email.
